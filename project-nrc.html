National Robotics Challenge

2024 – 2025 Chief Engineer, 15 person team Gold Award Honda Innovation Award

Overview

I led a team in the Manufacturing Workcell category at the National Robotics Challenge. Our answer was a fully autonomous 3D print farm that handled the whole printing process with nobody in the room: two open source Voron printers, a Cartesian gantry, and a plate swapping system that moved finished prints onto a shelf and started the next job.

It took the Gold Award and the Honda Innovation Award in our category at the National Robotics Challenge 2025.

My contributions

01 Creation and management of the digital twin

I built and maintained a live Onshape model of every subsystem, so the mechanical, electrical, and software groups all referenced one source of truth instead of three. Configurable aluminum extrusion and hardware libraries kept the unique part count down and made BOM generation quick, and sub-assemblies kept the main workspace organized and the load times survivable.

02 Cartesian gantry system

Designed, modeled, assembled, and tested a Cartesian gantry using a dual leadscrew Z axis and a belt driven X axis, both stepper driven, with limit switches on each end for leveling and homing. The X axis carries a custom screw tensioner for the GT2 belt so motion stayed consistent across a long competition day. Aluminum V groove extrusions and V wheels serve as the linear guides.

03 Pick-and-place mechanism

A forklift style mechanism that pulls finished prints off the bed. I ran oversized build plates so a strip of each plate hangs off the side of the heater. The forklift inserts from both sides and, using embedded magnets plus the gantry's Z motion, lifts the plate and whatever is printed on it clear of the printer. The overhanging strip then rests on the plate holders as the gantry drops it into the shelf.

The extension uses a two stage belt drive to maximize reach, on drawer slides picked for their thin profile and resistance to bending. The magnets removed the need for mechanical grippers entirely, which made the whole thing simpler and more reliable at the same time.

04 Shelving units

Designed, modeled, assembled, and tested the shelving for the stacked printer assembly, housing two Voron 2.4 printers and the plate holders, built from aluminum extrusion for rigidity and easy assembly. The back left array of plate holders hides a limit switch in every rung, so the control software always knows whether a plate was actually deposited or actually picked up rather than assuming it.

05 Running the team

I built and maintained a GitHub Projects workspace with a live Gantt chart. One view aligned deliverables across the hardware and software sub-teams, documented tasks, and surfaced blockers early enough to do something about them, which is what kept 15 people on schedule.
