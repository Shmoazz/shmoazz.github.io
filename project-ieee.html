IEEE SoutheastCon Robot

2023 – 2024 Mechanical Lead 12 x 12 x 12 in envelope 1st, Hardware Design

Overview

I played a crucial role in the design and fabrication of our robot for IEEE SoutheastCon 2024. It posted the highest scoring run out of 35 participating universities and won first place in the Hardware Design Competition.

The course had to be run with complete autonomy. The robot needed to handle three kinds of object, small blocks, big blocks, and small cylinders called thrusters, and cross a 7 inch wide, 2 inch descending gap on the way. All of it had to fold into a 12 x 12 x 12 inch envelope, so every mechanism was a volume argument before it was anything else.

My contributions

01 Chassis

Designed, modeled, and assembled a 10 x 10 inch chassis, mostly VEX metal, packing four DC motors and the electronics into the smallest footprint that still left room to work. It is the foundation everything else bolts to, so it had to leave clean interfaces for the bridge deployment, the thruster pickup, and the block manipulators without giving up any of the envelope.

02 Bridge

A 3D printed bridge and the mechanism that lays it, for a 7 inch wide, 2 inch descending gap. The placement device carries an embedded gear driven by a 360 degree servo through a pinion, and the bridge hangs on a keyhole slot, so a turn of the servo horn releases it cleanly instead of dragging it along.

03 Thruster pickup

A four bar linkage that stows completely inside the chassis and uses a parallel four bar motion to deploy out of the frame and down onto the thrusters. Servo torque was the constraint, so the crank runs through a 3:1 reduction on another embedded 3D printed gear.

The holder on the end of the linkage is a rectangular prism with three cylindrical cutouts, flared at the bottom to guide intake. Next to each hole sits a small servo with a custom horn that clamps the thruster once it is in.

04 Small blocks

I did not design the original small blocks intake. I was handed it to refine and to get inside the 12 inch envelope, so I added a mechanism that folds it up into the chassis and remade and shielded the rubber band holders, which were the part most likely to fail in the middle of a run.

Technologies

  • VEX metal structure
  • DC motor drivetrain
  • 3D printed custom mechanisms
  • Servo actuation
  • Fusion 360
  • Four bar linkages
  • Custom gearing for torque
  • Embedded microcontroller, fully autonomous
