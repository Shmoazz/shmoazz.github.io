Micron Precision Printer

2024 – 2025 Senior capstone ±1 µm target Mesa + LinuxCNC

Overview

A senior capstone build of a research grade 3D printer accurate to ±1 micron. The motion platform runs preloaded ground ball screws, 1 micron linear scales, and high torque steppers under LinuxCNC. A pneumatic extruder lays down a silver filled polymer, which is what makes the machine interesting: it prints conductive traces directly onto the part.

My team's job was the XYZ stage. Make the motion rock solid, and the materials group gets to spend their time on functional circuitry instead of chasing our slop.

My contributions

01 System level wiring diagram

The master wiring diagram covering motion control, limit switches, linear scales, and pneumatic valves. The color coded harness map made assembly fast and debugging survivable, and every reference cross links to its LinuxCNC HAL pin, so the document answers questions instead of raising them.

02 Electrical schematic

Detailed schematics for the Mesa 7i96 and 7i85 stack, the stepper drivers, the scale inputs, and the solenoid control. Ferrite beads and a star ground layout went in to tame electrical noise, which stops being a nicety once you are chasing micron level repeatability: at that scale the noise floor is the spec.

Technologies

  • LinuxCNC on a PREEMPT-RT kernel
  • Mesa 7i96 motion controller
  • Mesa 7i85 encoder board
  • C5 ground ball screws, zero backlash nuts
  • Heidenhain 1 micron linear scales
  • DM556T stepper drivers at 128x microstep
  • Pneumatic extrusion of conductive polymer

Results

What it did

Test moves confirmed ±1 micron repeatability over 100 mm of travel, and the printer successfully deposited conductive traces, which proved the motion system and the extrusion timing together rather than one at a time. By close-out I could generate working LinuxCNC configs from scratch, draft clean schematics, and produce wiring diagrams somebody else could actually follow.
